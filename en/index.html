01  Why now

This isn't about privacy. It's about who holds the plug.

I'm not a doomsayer and this is no conspiracy story. But there are a few facts you should simply know before you put your entire business with one supplier.

The CLOUD Act applies here too

A US law from 2018 obliges American companies to hand over data to American authorities, even when that data sits in a European data centre. Where the server physically stands matters less than who owns it.

Access can disappear through no fault of yours

Sanctions, export restrictions and policy changes are no longer theory. You don't have to do anything wrong to be affected — you only have to end up on the wrong side of a line someone else draws.

The price is no longer the price

Per user, per month, per feature. Cloud subscriptions rarely go down, and migrating gets more expensive the longer you stay. That isn't an accident, that's the business model.

03  The technology

Why NixOS, and why that matters to you.

Most self-built servers turn into a mystery after two years: nobody knows exactly what is on them any more, or why it works. NixOS solves that structurally, and that's the reason I build on it.

The whole server lives in one text file

Every setting, every program, every user: it sits in configuration you can read, keep under version control and show to your accountant or auditor. There are no manual tweaks that only exist in someone's head.

Rolling back an update takes one reboot

If an update goes wrong, you pick the previous version at boot and you're running again. On ordinary servers that's an evening of work; here it's a choice in a menu.

If the server burns down, I rebuild it

From that same file the exact same server rolls out onto new hardware. Your backups are then only needed for your data, not to reconstruct how the thing was put together.

No supplier locking you in

Everything is open software. If you want to continue with someone else tomorrow, you hand that person the configuration and the keys. There is no contract holding your data hostage.

06  Honestly

When this is a bad idea.

I'd rather not sell this to someone who ends up regretting it, so here it is plainly.

If nobody at your company wants to touch technology

Your own server needs attention now and then. With a maintenance contract I take that over, but if you truly want to never think about it, a European cloud provider is honestly the better choice.

If you're tied to software that only runs in their cloud

If your whole business runs on one package that can't work outside that environment, you're moving the problem instead of solving it. We look at that in the scan, before you spend anything.

If there are fewer than three of you

Then you rarely earn the cost back. For a one-person business a good European provider plus solid backups is usually more sensible than your own hardware.

07  Questions

What people usually ask.

What if the server breaks?

Disks always come in pairs or more, so one failed disk stops nothing. Your backups sit at a second location. And because the entire setup is fixed in configuration, the exact same thing is running again on new hardware within a day.

So where does that server stand?

Wherever you want. At your office, or in a European data centre with a European provider. In that second case you rent the machine, but the keys stay yours — the host can't get into your data.

Am I then dependent on you instead of on Microsoft?

A fair question. That's why you get everything: keys, configuration and documentation. Any administrator who can handle Linux can take it over. I want you to stay because you're happy, not because you're stuck.

Is this legally fine under GDPR?

Data you keep under your own control inside the EU makes it demonstrably simpler — you have no transfers to third countries to justify. I deliver the technical side and the documentation for it. For your processing register and legal texts you still need a lawyer; I'm not one.

How long does it take?

A scan takes a week. For a basic setup I count on three to four weeks from go-ahead to running, most of which is migration and running in parallel. Your old system keeps working the whole time.

And if you get hit by a bus tomorrow?

Then your server simply keeps running — there is no service of mine keeping it up. The configuration sits with you, in plain readable files. That's exactly why I build it this way.
